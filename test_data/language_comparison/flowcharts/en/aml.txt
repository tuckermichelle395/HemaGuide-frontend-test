English structured treatment flowchart - AML / APL
Scope: English rendering of the local AML treatment pathway used for language-controlled testing.
The pathway is a structured summary of the version-controlled local guideline flowchart; it is not a verbatim guideline and does not replace clinical judgment.

General principles:
- Confirm disease entity, treatment phase, genetic risk, MRD status, fitness, organ function and transplant eligibility.
- Integrate morphology, immunophenotype, cytogenetics, fusion testing and molecular testing.
- Consider clinical trials whenever available; account for drug access, transplant resources and patient preferences.
- Treatment recommendations must address induction or low-intensity therapy, response assessment, consolidation, MRD monitoring, transplant and supportive care.

Branch AML-0: Information integration and risk stratification
Applicable when an AML/APL case enters treatment decision-making.
Key checks: disease entity, genetic risk, treatment phase, fitness, urgent complications and an actionable MRD marker.
Recommended path: resolve missing diagnostic information before committing to a treatment pathway.

Branch AML-1: Suspected or confirmed APL
Start urgent ATRA when APL is strongly suspected; manage coagulopathy and differentiation syndrome risk while confirming PML::RARA.

Branch AML-5: Newly diagnosed non-APL AML, fit for intensive therapy
Use genetic risk, core-binding-factor status, actionable mutations and MRD strategy to select induction and post-remission treatment.

Branch AML-7: Newly diagnosed AML with FLT3 alteration
Discuss an FLT3-directed strategy where indicated and define MRD monitoring and transplant planning.

Branch AML-8: Therapy-related, MDS-related, complex-karyotype, TP53-mutated or otherwise adverse-risk AML
Integrate disease biology, fitness, clinical-trial access and transplant intent; avoid treating age as the sole intensity criterion.

Branch AML-9: Not fit for intensive therapy or declines intensive therapy
Consider lower-intensity therapy, supportive care and clinical trials according to fitness, comorbidity, disease biology and patient goals.

Branch AML-10: Persistent disease or induction failure
Reassess morphology, genetics and MRD; consider salvage therapy, targeted treatment and transplant or trial pathways.

Branch AML-11: Consolidation and maintenance after complete remission
Use genetic risk, MRD and transplant eligibility to determine consolidation, maintenance and allogeneic transplant strategy.

Branch AML-12: Relapsed or refractory non-APL AML
Classify relapse type, repeat or review genetics and actionable targets, and select targeted therapy, salvage therapy, clinical trial or supportive care.

Branch AML-12A: Relapsed/refractory AML output principle
Applicable to relapsed/refractory AML or failure of multiple prior lines.
Recommended path:
- Recheck genetics and actionable targets, especially FLT3, IDH1, IDH2, NPM1, TP53, CD33 and MRD markers.
- If an actionable alteration is present, prioritize targeted therapy or a clinical trial as disease control or a bridge to transplant.
- If no targeted option is available, select intensive or lower-intensity salvage according to fitness and prior therapy.
- State whether the goal is curative bridging, response induction, survival prolongation or symptom control.

Branch AML-13: Insufficient or conflicting information
State uncertainty explicitly, identify the missing data and avoid overconfident treatment selection.

Branch AML-14: AML MRD pathway
Define a disease-specific MRD marker, monitor at clinically meaningful time points and integrate MRD dynamics with genetic risk and transplant decisions.
